Seven executable attacks · updated 30 August 2026

Prediction is not the conclusion.

The useful thread survived, but the grand reading did not. History can disambiguate a local present. Closing a predictor into a loop creates dynamics. Neither fact gives us agency, intelligence, or a new learning principle. The harder question is now: which evidence should the system pay to obtain, and what survives long enough for consequence to teach that choice?

What survived

Persistent, addressable state changes what a local signal can mean. A temporary trace can keep delayed evidence reachable. The same mechanism can train a conditional decision to acquire another observation.

What did not

Prediction loss is not motivation. A closed rollout is not autonomy. “Local” does not solve biological credit assignment. More observation is not automatically better when sensing has a cost.

The argument so far

Each gate attacks the previous claim.

The project is not a ladder of confirmations. Every gate adds the boring attacker, hidden assumption, or resource bill that the preceding result made easy to ignore.

GATE 0

The present needs history

A receiver-carried trace resolves clockwise versus counter-clockwise motion when the current pulse alone cannot.

Attack: an explicit previous-position Markov rule performs just as well.
GATE 1

Prediction changes its data

A prediction-only output gate suppresses roughly 95% of possible motion and makes its future easy to predict.

Correction: low loss can mean a frozen world, not a better model.
GATE 2

Relevance arrives late

A bounded fast trace preserves detail until the system learns what matters, then projects only that fragment into slow state.

Bill: the trace buys time with temporary storage.
GATE 3

Context can be reinstated

A noisy cue retrieves the temporal context of its earlier encounter with ordinary key-value episodic attention.

Territory: this is content-addressed memory, not a novel hippocampal mechanism.
GATE 4

Learn what to retain

A learned filter spends scarce slow memory better than fixed novelty or recency rules when future relevance is predictable.

Hidden gift: training received outcomes for retained and discarded events.
GATE 5

Selection hides correction

With feedback only for retained events, a successful filter can become a self-sealing observation mask under drift.

Requirement: exploration, side information, or a trace must restore observation support.
GATE 6

Sensing has a price

A delayed audit plus a surviving trial trace learns when another observation is worth buying and reallocates sensing after drift.

Attacker: the oracle is a one-line value-of-information threshold.
Gate 6 · active sensing

Misconceptions removed

A smaller claim is a stronger one.

The project becomes more interesting when the metaphors stop doing unpaid work.

“Prediction lives inside the pulse.”

Metaphor only. The pulse is the current state. Each addressed cell carries parameters and a decaying trace used to predict its next local activation.

“Local error is biologically local.”

Not established. The next activation is supplied as a teaching target. No backpropagation through time is used, but general biological credit assignment remains unsolved.

“Prediction creates purpose.”

Gate 1 rejects this. A controller rewarded only for predictability learns to suppress motion. Viability, exploration, and consequence must enter explicitly.

“A learned filter keeps learning.”

Only from reachable evidence. Gate 4 worked under full feedback. Gate 5 shows that selected-only feedback can make its compression policy self-confirming.

“Surprise reveals hidden change.”

Only on observed support. Surprise can report that the selected region worsened. It cannot reveal that an unobserved region improved while selected outcomes stayed identical.

“Fast trace is free memory.”

It has a receipt. Gate 5 pays 80 temporary slots for full feedback; Gate 6 pays 60 scalars to bridge a 12-trial audit delay. Time is bought with capacity.

“More observation is always better.”

Not when sensing costs. Gate 6's always-sense arm is highly accurate but loses 0.0381 late utility to the oracle's conditional probe policy.

Next attack · Gate 7

Put the body under an edge budget.

The proposed slow-body × fast-conductance factorization now needs a matched topology test. “Dendritic,” “fractal,” and “local” earn nothing unless their sparse support produces useful state-conditioned operators per persistent edge.

same persistent-edge budget ├─ local lattice ├─ random sparse ├─ small-world ├─ multiscale / dyadic └─ learned support ↓ fast state chooses current conductance ↓ task score + routing cost + attacker
  1. Equalize parameters and edges: topology does not get a hidden capacity gift.
  2. Vary dependency range: local and long-range tasks should favor different supports.
  3. Attack the metaphor: compare against a small state-space model and sparse attention.
  4. Charge adaptation: count rewiring, fast routing state, and persistent storage separately.
